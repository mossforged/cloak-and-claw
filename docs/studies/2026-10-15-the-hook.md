# What makes our game distinct? A study of hooks

*Study, 2026-10-15. Speculative by design. Nothing here is decided; choices go to `docs/decisions.md`.*

## Where the project stood

- **Stage 2** in progress (shield block T007, spin slash T008 queued; Rat Raider rig parts A004 with the art builder). Stage 1 passed; the Stage 3 rig is done.
- **Decided so far:** belt-scroller, arcade run with River City Ransom-style growth (later branching + boons), block by default, throws, floor weapons and breakables, lives, about 120 px target belt, co-op for up to three, an assembly line of body x colour x weapon kit x gear.
- **Parked:** League-style hero kits, companion/pet, a 360 spin special, the enemy design workspace.
- **The question:** the user ruled out mounts (animals in this world rarely ride) and asked what will make *our* game distinct: co-op, League-style abilities, species x class, or something else.

---

## 1. What a hook is (and the test we'll hold every idea to)

Every genre classic is remembered for one or two ideas that change how **every fight** plays (genre study, section 7). A hook is not a feature list. Our test:

1. **One sentence:** can you pitch it in one line that makes someone want to play?
2. **Every fight:** does it change what you do minute to minute, not just between stages?
3. **Pillars:** every hit lands; fair crowds; you grow by fighting; the art stays doable.
4. **Taste** (the user's): co-op against the game or direct conflict; characters that feel distinct and change during a run; decisions that matter; no fiddly bookkeeping; theme drives mechanics.
5. **Ours:** does it come out of *this* world (animals as peoples, a compressed size scale, heroic and warm) rather than being bolted on?
6. **Cheap to test:** can we prove it fun with art we have, before committing?

## 2. Hooks that made other games distinct

| Game | The hook | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|
| Double Dragon | Weapons from enemies; co-op friendly fire | The floor and your partner become tools (and jokes) |
| Golden Axe | Mounts; magic pots; thieves | Fantasy chaos; something to chase |
| River City Ransom | Open town; shops; food raises stats; books teach moves | Growth feels personal and funny |
| The Simpsons (arcade) | Team-up moves when partners stand together | Co-op as a mechanic, not just two players |
| Knights of the Round | Level up mid-run | Arcade + growth |
| D&D Shadow over Mystara | Six classes, spells, inventory, branching paths | A tabletop party in 45 minutes |
| Guardian Heroes | Three depth lanes; karma; branching story | Choices with consequences |
| Sengoku series (as recalled) | Transform into different warriors mid-fight | One player, several play styles |
| Marvel vs Capcom (fighting game) | Call a partner in for an assist on cooldown | A party behind one player |
| Castle Crashers | Many unlockable knights; animal pets that help | Collecting and companions |
| Dragon's Crown | Classes, loot, rune spells, AI party members from bones you find | Solo play still feels like a party |
| Way of the Passive Fist | You only defend (parry and counter) | Inverts the genre's verb |
| Charlie Murder | Punk band; RPG; tattoos; a social-media phone | Personality and style |
| Streets of Rage 4 | Special moves cost health you win back by attacking | Rewards aggression |
| Dungeon Fighter Online | Classes with cooldown skill bars and ultimates | Deep build identity |
| Absolum | Roguelite runs; boons; parry | Replay from recombination |
| Wildermyth (not a brawler; the user's reference) | Heroes change through story: scars, transformations, relationships | Characters you remember |

Two patterns stand out:
- **Party fantasy:** Mystara, Dragon's Crown and Marvel vs Capcom make one player feel like a team.
- **One inverted or doubled verb:** parry-only, health-as-fuel, transform mid-fight.

## 3. The candidates, honestly assessed

### A. Co-op by itself
Every modern brawler has 2–4 player co-op. On its own it **isn't distinct**. It becomes distinct when the team has mechanics only a team can do (see C and D).
*Verdict: necessary, not a hook.*

### B. League-style kits (passive + three cooldown abilities + an ultimate)
Strong hero identity and clear co-op roles (tank, damage, support). Dungeon Fighter did it, but few Western brawlers have. Risks: buttons (keyboard and phone), and balancing kits is real work. It fits the user's love of League.
*Verdict: a strong ingredient; on its own, "Dungeon Fighter with animals".*

### C. Species x class: build your hero
- **Species** sets the body and one innate trait. For example: a mouse is small (slips under high swings, light, fast); a badger is heavy (can't be knocked down mid-swing, slow); a squirrel jumps high and hangs from things; a hare dashes; a hedgehog's spines hurt whoever grabs it.
- **Class** is a **weapon kit**: fighter, archer, mage, rogue.
- **Fits our tech perfectly:** it's exactly the body x weapon kit assembly line. One rig per species, kits shared across species.
- **Fits the world:** species are peoples with their own ways, and the compressed size scale makes size meaningful.
- **Risk:** combinations multiply balancing. Keep species traits to one each and kits small, and let bots check.

*Verdict: the most "ours" option, and cheap to scale.*

### D. Size as a team mechanic
In a compressed-scale world, a badger is twice a mouse's height. That can matter in fights:
- **Team throws:** a big hero hurls a small ally across the belt as a living cannonball (Castle Crashers wanted throwable partners and cut it).
- **Small heroes** slip between legs to hit a big enemy's back, or get under high swings.
- **Big heroes** can't be thrown; small ones can be grabbed by big enemies, and a partner has to free them.
- Enemies of different sizes ask different questions (a beetle you can't throw, rats you can).

*Verdict: distinctive and thematic; works best combined with C.*

### E. Your party, even solo (swap and assist)
You bring a **party of three**. Solo, you control one and **tag-swap** to another at any time (switching kit and size mid-fight), or **call one in** for a quick assist on a cooldown (Marvel vs Capcom). In co-op, each player takes one member. That answers "what does co-op add": in solo you're juggling a team; in co-op your friends *are* the team.
- **Fits:** co-op against the game (taste), the planned three-player co-op, companion/pet (an AI party member is the companion), and C (a party mixes species and classes). Assists are a natural home for League-style ultimates.
- **Cost:** AI for benched partners only during assists (simple); one rig per party member (the assembly line).

*Verdict: a strong spine that turns A, B, C and D into one idea.*

### F. Mercy: enemies are people
In this world rats, stoats and the rest are peoples, not monsters. When an enemy is beaten, a choice: knock them out (normal) or **spare** them. Spared enemies flee. Later they might return the favour, sell to you, or warn others, so fewer fight. Or some could join the party.
- **Distinct:** almost no brawler does this. It fits a warm, heroic tone and gives meaning to fights.
- **Risks:** it can slow the arcade flow, and a "kill vs spare" choice can turn into bookkeeping. Keep it to one button on a downed enemy, with consequences between stages, not mid-fight.

*Verdict: a distinctive flavour layer; strong for story, optional for the core loop.*

### G. Feasts instead of shops
River City Ransom's food that raises stats, recast as **camp feasts between stages**. Ingredients come from crates and enemies; cooking a dish gives the whole party a buff for the next stage. Food is central to the cosy-heroic animal fantasy tradition, and the art bible already has pie and cheese.
*Verdict: a charming home for the RPG layer (Stage 6). Flavour, not the core hook.*

### H. Double-edged trinkets
Every power-up has a side effect, for example strong attacks but a growing temper that makes you hard to stop. It's the roguelite boon layer (direction C in the genre study) with a twist that makes choices interesting.
*Verdict: a good home for the later roguelite step.*

### I. Heroes who change (the Wildermyth idea)
Over a run, heroes pick up scars, gear and titles that **show on the rig** (gear layers and part swaps), plus a small trait. It's cheap with our parts system, and memorable.
*Verdict: a lovely long-term layer; not first.*

### Ruled out or deprioritised
- **Mounts:** the user's call. A riding animal doesn't fit a world where animals are the people. (A "battle cart" or a war-boar pulled by allies could be a one-off set piece, but no.)
- **A parry-only game** (Way of the Passive Fist style): too narrow for a party brawler, but parry as a skill layer on block stays possible.

## 4. Three coherent pitches

**Pitch 1: "The Fellowship" (recommended to test first).**
*"Build a party of three animal heroes, each a different species and class, and swap or call them in mid-fight. Play solo as a whole party, or with friends who each take a hero."*
Core: E (party swap/assist) + C (species x class) + D (size team moves). Later: B (kits for each class), G (feasts), I (heroes change).

**Pitch 2: "Kit Brawler".**
*"A League-style hero brawler: each animal hero has a passive, three abilities and an ultimate; team up with friends in roles."*
Core: B + co-op roles. Fewer, deeper heroes. Distinct mainly through hero design.

**Pitch 3: "The Merciful Blade".**
*"A warm-hearted brawler where your enemies are people: spare them and the world changes."*
Core: F + G + I. Story and tone carry it; combat stays classic.

These combine: Pitch 1 can carry simple kits (one cooldown ability and an ultimate per class, borrowed from Pitch 2) and a spare-or-defeat choice (from Pitch 3) without losing focus.

## 5. How we'd prove it cheaply (before committing)

Using art we already have (the art bible's Badger Guardian and Squirrel Ranger standees):
1. **Assist test:** press a key and the Badger Guardian standee leaps in, does one heavy slam, and leaps out (cooldown about 8 s). Does calling a friend feel great?
2. **Swap test:** swap between Mouse Fighter and a Badger standee mid-fight (different speed, damage, weight). Does switching style mid-crowd feel good or confusing?
3. **Size test:** the badger throws the mouse across the belt as a projectile. Funny and useful, or gimmick?

Each is a small code task and needs no new art. One playtest answers whether Pitch 1 is the spine.

## User's reaction (2026-10-15)
- Hook tests are a **later** thing, not now.
- Likes ally call-ins and swapping between two heroes, with the benched hero slotted to do one specific assist: "pretty cool... something to return to."
- Concern: solo call-ins and co-op partners "feel like very different systems." If co-op lets players help each other, solo should have the same: **an ally or two on screen, controlled by AI.**
- "I don't think I like the Merciful Blade [/] kit brawler. I will have to think about." Confirmed by the user: Merciful Blade is out; Kit Brawler undecided. All of this is for much later, once core systems are locked down.

**Lead's note on reconciling it:** one system for both modes: the party is always **three heroes on screen**; in solo, AI drives the other two, and each friend who joins takes over an AI slot. Swap/assist then becomes a per-player layer on top (each player has an active hero and one benched partner with a slotted assist). Main risk: good **ally AI** is harder than enemy AI (allies must help without stealing the fight or getting in the way).

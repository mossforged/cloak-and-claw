## Views (what the study missed)
A **view** is the direction the chest (torso) or hips (pelvis) face. Each view has its own drawing, its own joint points (where the neck, shoulders, hips and tail attach on that drawing) and its own **draw order** (which limbs are in front of the body). Chest and hips pick views separately: in a slash the chest turns further than the hips.

| View | Code | Torso | Pelvis | Head | Near arm | Far arm | Legs / tail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| three-quarter (default) | Q | have | have | neutral set | shoulder under the vest (590), forearm and hand in front (820s) | behind the body (100s) | as now |
| side (profile) | S | have (A018) | **new** | neutral set | **wholly in front** (800s), near shoulder cap on top | behind (100s) | as Q |
| turned toward camera | F | **new** | **new** | **front head, new** | wholly in front, near shoulder cap on top | behind, showing on the far side | tail behind the pelvis |
| turned away (back three-quarter) | BQ | **new** | Q or S | neutral set | **wholly behind the body** (wound back) | behind (100s) | as Q |
| back | B | have (A018) | **new** (seat and tail root toward camera) | have (head-back) | behind the body | **wholly in front** (800s), far shoulder cap on top | near and far legs swap depth; tail in front of the pelvis |

Turning = a chain of views, the depth switching on the frame where the hand passes the edge of the body. A turn-around (facing flip) passes through F for two frames with the scale crossing zero. Mirroring keeps one-sided details (eyepatch, pauldron) on the camera side, as every classic brawler does.

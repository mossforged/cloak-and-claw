## What was built
- **Who leads is data per handling type** (`src/data/item-lead.js`):
  - One-handers (cutlass, dagger, axe, club) keep today's behaviour: the item follows the hand.
  - Spear, staff and bow are item-led. A single move can override this.
- **Item-led solve** (`src/render/item-lead.js`):
  - A key poses the item itself: where the grip point sits relative to the hips or chest, and the shaft's angle.
  - Both fists then reach the user's grip lines on it inside the limits, with each wrist's range counted.
  - If the arms can't reach, the item moves the least distance that lets both hold. The angle stays as the key set it.
  - `itemLead` (0 to 1) fades between the arms' own pose and the item-led one.
- **One deviation from the packet:** the second fist may slide back along the shaft up to 16 px before the item moves. This is the same rule the existing two-hand grip already uses. Without it, the spear was forced up across the snout. It's still on the shaft, so no hand leaves its grip.
- **Impact** (`src/data/impact.js`):
  - On the hit frame, the pose carries 15% past the strike and the weapon arm is 6% longer along its bones.
  - On the next frame, the arm is 3% longer; then it settles.
  - It's on for v3's slash, chop, spear thrust, staff swing, staff overhead and shield bash.
  - The raider's own clips don't have it, so the seam and pass-bar tests, which only run the game rigs, are unaffected.
  - At game size the stretch is subtle; it shows best in the slowed-down viewer.
- **Lab sliders** for the impact numbers and for the slide and search distance. The defaults ship tested; nothing needs tuning.

## What it does
`node tools/joint-check.mjs <rig-source.json> [--joints <joints.json>] [--out <dir>] [--only a,b]`

- **Assembly:** it puts the pieces together at rest, using the source's pivots, or the joints file's points and ranges when one is given. It then turns each joint through its range in 9 samples, with every piece below it turning too.
- **What it checks:** a window around the joint point, about the width of the turning piece, for three faults. Each is measured against the rest pose, so it only counts what the turn creates:
  - **Gap / notch:** see-through pixels that a 10 px closing of the outline fills, and that sit within 10 px of both the turning piece and its neighbours in the rig tree. Including the neighbours catches the near-shoulder notch against the head; the closeness rule ignores the slit between a fist and the belly.
  - **Fragments:** thin detached visible bits, added up per sample.
  - **Seams:** dark outline pixels of the covered piece showing beside the piece over it, away from the outside edge, added up per sample.
  - **Islands:** each PNG's own detached bits.
- **Output:**
  - `joint-check.json`: pass or fail per joint and kind, the worst sample, every flagged angle, and a `peak` number (how close a passing joint came to the limit).
  - `joint-check.jpg`: a contact sheet about 950 px wide, one crop per flagged joint and kind, with the fault coloured in. Gap is magenta, fragment cyan, seam yellow, island orange.
  - It exits with code 1 if anything is flagged.
- **What it needs:** only Node and git. No browser, no Python, no npm packages; I added a small JPEG writer for the contact sheet. The calibration script also needs git-lfs to pull the old images out of history.
- **Speed:** about 6 s for the full 21-piece rat.

The limits that came out of calibration:
- **Gap:** flags one notch of 250 px or more.
- **Seam:** flags when a sample's total reaches 4 × the window radius.
- **Fragment:** flags when a sample's thin bits (3 px thick or less) total 1.2 × the window radius.

The tightest margin is the fragment limit against the accepted A026 ankle (share 0.82). A027 r1's neck jaggedness actually shows up as seams, not fragments: its scraps are about the same size as the shin bits peeking out of the accepted ankle's cuff. So the fragment check only catches breakup worse than that.
